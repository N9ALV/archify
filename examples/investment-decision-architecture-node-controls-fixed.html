Decision Principle

  • Evidence precedes action; price movement alone is not a thesis.
  • Separate opportunity quality from portfolio sizing.
  • Make invalidation conditions explicit before implementation.

Asymmetry Test

  • Compare expected upside, downside and probability—not forecast precision alone.
  • Reject attractive standalone ideas that worsen portfolio concentration or regime exposure.
  • Sizing is the final expression of conviction after risk constraints.

Feedback Loop

  • Monitor whether the causal thesis is strengthening, weakening or unchanged.
  • Re-evaluate when new evidence arrives or an invalidation condition is approached.
  • A valid replacement opportunity can be a reason to exit even without thesis failure.
